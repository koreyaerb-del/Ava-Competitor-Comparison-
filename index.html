Executive summary

Five things the market says about Ava

01

Ava owns open ground: applied technical skill, remotely, at volume

Of 23 competitors reviewed, none documents a remote interview that shows a candidate real equipment, readings or parts and scores what they do with it. AI interviewers check self-reported answers. Skills incumbents (Ramsay, SHL) use proctored multiple-choice tests. Amatrol proves skill with physical rigs that need a site visit.

02

Voice is now table stakes

HireVue launched a voice AI Interviewer in June 2026, Phenom in November 2025, Eightfold in October 2025. Ava should be sold on what it assesses, not on how it talks.

03

Cheap voice tools are moving into the trades

Ribbon targets manufacturing at $499/mo. Goodfit charges $5 an assessment. Classet calls trades applicants within seconds from $249/mo. They will anchor mid-market price expectations.

04

The real incumbent is a 50-year-old test

Ramsay’s proctored MAINTEST (2.5 hours, $46–71 per test) is how many plants screen maintenance techs today. Sell Ava as “Ramsay-plus”: the skills evidence buyers trust, without the proctor or the paper.

05

Trust is the enterprise gate, and Ava’s story is strong

No tone, accent or emotion analysis. A human decides. Four-year records. Those answer the lawsuits hitting Workday and Eightfold. Closing three gaps (SOC 2 report, published bias audit, fraud/ID checks) would put Ava level with the enterprise leaders.

Battlecards

The six head-to-heads Ava will face

Who shows up in a manufacturing deal, what they’ll claim, and the question that moves the buyer toward Ava.

HireVue

Enterprise incumbent
Their pitch
Validated science (1,300+ validity studies), 60%+ of the Fortune 100, and now a voice AI Interviewer (June 2026).
They win
Brand, procurement familiarity, breadth of assessment types.
Ava wins
Built for technicians. HireVue’s manufacturing pages have no trade content, and its video legacy carries candidate skepticism.

Ask the buyer: “Show me the HireVue question that tells you whether a maintenance tech can read a meter.”

Eightfold

Enterprise platform
Their pitch
Talent intelligence plus an AI Interviewer (Oct 2025) and the strongest certifications in the field: SOC 2 Type II, ISO 42001, FedRAMP Moderate.
They win
Compliance paperwork and an existing platform footprint.
Ava wins
Eightfold evaluates “only what candidates say.” Ava evaluates what they do with what they see. Per Tim, TAP beat Eightfold head-to-head.

Ask the buyer: “Would you hire an electrician on what they said, or on how they read the panel?” Note: Eightfold faces an FCRA suit over hidden match scores (Jan 2026).

Paradox (Workday)

Frontline at scale
Their pitch
Olivia handles apply, screen and schedule by chat and SMS in 100+ languages, bundled with Workday.
They win
Already inside the customer’s Workday contract; scheduling depth.
Ava wins
Olivia runs knockout questions and logistics. It never assesses skill. Ava is the skills layer that sits after Olivia.

Position: not either/or. “Keep Olivia for scheduling. Add Ava so the people Olivia schedules can actually do the job.”

Ribbon

Closest overlap
Their pitch
Voice interviews for manufacturing, warehousing and trades. Public pricing from $499/mo, 60+ ATS integrations, NYC bias audit done.
They win
Price transparency, self-serve setup, a published audit.
Ava wins
Ribbon asks questions. Ava shows equipment and scores the answer against a 0/1/2 rubric. A spoken answer can be rehearsed; reading a gauge in a photo cannot.

Ask the buyer: “What’s a bad technician hire cost you? Then $499 a month isn’t the number to optimize.”

Classet & Goodfit

Low-cost trades voice
Their pitch
Classet calls applicants within seconds (from $249/mo). Goodfit charges $5 an assessment with employer-written question pools.
They win
Speed-to-lead and price for small and mid-size shops.
Ava wins
Classet confirms self-reported credentials and doesn’t verify them. Goodfit relies on the employer writing good questions. Ava generates job-specific scenarios from the job description.

Segment note: these will win the 10-tech shop. Ava’s fight is the plant hiring hundreds of technicians a year.

Ramsay Corporation

The status quo
Their pitch
Fifty years of norms. MAINTEST covers 21 mechanical and electrical areas in 153 items; tests from $28 to $71.
They win
Defensibility HR already trusts, and a low price per test.
Ava wins
On-demand on a phone instead of 2.5 proctored hours; applied scenarios instead of recall; no testing room or proctor.

Position: “Ramsay-plus.” Run Ava upstream to screen the funnel, keep Ramsay for finalists, and compare results in the pilot.

Pricing & science

What buyers pay, and what actually predicts performance

Published price per assessment

Only four vendors in this field publish prices. Ava and every enterprise vendor quote custom.

Ribbon: $499/mo for 100 interviews down to $1,999/mo for 1,000. Ramsay: aptitude and PLC tests about $28; MAINTEST $46–71 by volume. Classet bills per completed interview from $249/mo (unit price not public).

Predictors of job performance

Sackett et al. (2022), corrected validity. Ava combines the top three in one step.

Structured interviews ranked first. The 2022 study revised Schmidt & Hunter (1998), which had put cognitive ability on top. Validity is the correlation with later job performance.

Implication for pricing. Ramsay proves enterprise buyers pay $46–71 per technician test today. Voice tools are anchoring at $2–5 per interview. Ava should be priced on the cost of a bad technician hire and sold on outcomes (time-to-fill, 90-day retention), not compared per interview with tools that only record answers.

Regulation

The rules every enterprise buyer will raise

AI hiring rules are stacking up state by state. Each one is easier to pass with Ava’s design: the same job-based scenarios for everyone, audio only, no emotion analysis, a human deciding.

Colorado and EU dates changed in 2026 and come from secondary legal summaries; confirm before quoting to a customer. Illinois’s 2020 video-interview law applies only if video is analyzed; Ava records audio only, per its privacy policy.

How I’d sell against this field

Six plays for a founding AE

Show, don’t tell

Every competitor demos a conversation. I’d hand the plant manager a phone and let him take the conveyor question himself. The demo is the differentiator.

Sell “Ramsay-plus” to the status quo

Don’t ask buyers to rip out the test they trust. Pilot Ava upstream, keep Ramsay for finalists, and let the pilot data make the case.

Partner with the ATS, never fight it

Workday/Paradox and SmartRecruiters/Winston own the system of record. Ava is the skills layer that feeds it. SmartRecruiters is already a live integration.

Win procurement on trust

Lead with no emotion analysis, human decisions and four-year records. Build the security and legal packet once, then reuse it in every deal.

Price on the cost of a bad hire

Anchor to downtime, scrap and 90-day turnover, not dollars per interview. That keeps Ava out of the $5 race and closer to what enterprises already pay for testing.

Target where the platforms leave a gap

Plants running HireVue or Paradox still have no technician skills screen. Those are warm accounts: budget, AI comfort and an open problem.

What I’d bring back to product from the field

  • Spanish first. Much of the West’s technician workforce needs it, and every major competitor offers 30+ languages.
  • Fraud and identity checks. Gartner expects 1 in 4 candidate profiles to be fake by 2028.
  • A public integrations page and a SOC 2 report to shorten security review.
  • A published bias-audit summary to match Harver, Sapia, Ribbon and Classet.
